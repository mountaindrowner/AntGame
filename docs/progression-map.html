Ant family trees

Every new ant grows out of one you already have, up to four generations deep. The label above each ant is what unlocks it: a boss's trophy, a room, an evolution node, or a great evolution (gold). Tap an ant to jump to its upgrades.

Veteran ranks

Civilization's unit experience, for ants. Every ant earns experience by doing its job and climbs five ranks. You can see rank on the ant itself: new ants hatch pale (real ants do; they're called callows) and darken as they age, and the best carry a glint and a name. Losing veterans hurts, so keeping them alive matters.

Earning experience

Promotions (picked once per ant type, not per ant)

With hundreds of ants, picking for each would be a chore. Instead you set a doctrine for each ant type: one tap in its sheet says which promotion its Veterans take, and which second one its Elites add.

Keeping and making veterans

    Where it comes from

    • Civilization V and VI: units gain experience and pick promotions from a list for their class; a Barracks makes new units start with experience.
    • XCOM: soldiers earn ranks and names, and a memorial wall remembers the fallen. Losing a veteran stings.
    • Real ants: experienced foragers do measurably better, and workers change jobs as they age (nurses first, foragers later).

    Upgrades for each ant

    Bought with materials (never 🧬 Genes), once for the whole ant type. Level 1 is a boost; levels 2 and 3 add a boost and a permanent choice of two abilities. The four core ants (Forager, Builder, Soldier, Nurse) go two levels further: level 4 is a big boost and level 5 is a gold mastery choice that changes how the colony plays.

    The queen

    Her level sets how many ants the colony can hold; each satellite queen adds 20 more. Levels VII and VIII are new. At IV, VI and VIII she takes a permanent trait.

    Evolution tree

    Five branches, paid in 🧬 Genes plus materials. Genes come from playing: 1 per 10 eggs hatched, 1 per new region, 1 per new kind of thing brought home, 2–5 per boss, 1–2 per goal, 5 per rival queen. Lines show what must come first; the thick boxes at the bottom of each branch are the expensive late nodes. A yard run affords about half the tree, so every colony is different. Each node has a deed that halves its cost; any node but the lineage can be refunded.

    Great evolutions

    Huge, one-time choices, like Civilization's wonders and Stellaris's ascension perks. Each copies a real ant species' way of life, changes how you play, and shows in the yard. A colony gets three, out of twelve, and some rule each other out, so no two colonies are alike.

    When it all arrives

    The same pieces by play time. Read down a column for what a player is doing then; read across to watch one track grow. Swipe sideways on a phone.

    Materials

    One new material every 30 to 60 minutes, each with its own way to get it and its own catch.
