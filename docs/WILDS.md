## 1. The shape of it

**The wilds are the yard's outermost regions, inside the fence:** the three sides farthest from the house. The yard is a 10 × 8 grid with the house along the top. The wilds are its west column, east column and south row (21 regions), the last ground before the fence:

| Side | Regions now | Zone and guardian now |
|---|---|---|
| West | side yard ×3, the shed, the thistles | the Side Yard (the black widow); the thistles are the Wet Edge's |
| East | the hedge ×6 | the Hedge Row (the praying mantis) |
| South | toad hole, reeds, slugs, drain, mudflat, wet edge, the leaf piles, the fire ant country | the Wet Edge (Old Warty), the Rot (the giant centipede), Fire Ant Country (the fire ants' queen) |

**No new ground is needed.** The yard, its zones and their guardians stay as they are: Phase 1 still happens across the whole yard, edges included. **The wilds are a second tier on the outer ring:** once you have three colonies, the edges *stir*. The things that live closest to the fence, nearest the surfacing glow, wake up, and the ring's rules turn wild. The Oak's Crown (zone 4) is the exception: inland, reached up the Oak's trunk.

**What makes them wild:**
- **The edges turn hostile.** Once the edges stir, the outer ring's hazards come back at full strength, whatever guardians you've beaten there, until that side's wild boss falls. Everything there is bigger and hungrier.
- **No trails stick.** Wind, water and rubble scatter scent: trails fade four times as fast. You need outposts as relays.
- **Long supply lines.** The ring is as far from the central nest as the yard goes. Without an outpost or a daughter close by, foraging there doesn't pay.
- **The glow comes nearer the surface out here.** The ore was always underground (Mark: "the ore was already underground"); under the middle of the yard it sits at the bedrock, but toward the fence it rises, and at the edges the seams break the surface in places. The things that live and dig there have changed. The wilds hold the only surface seams.
- **Danger rises with the glow.** Ground over an open seam hurts ants who stay too long (the deferred "stored glowrock is dangerous" idea, moved outdoors where it's a place, not a punishment).

## 2. Five wild lairs on the ring (and the Oak)

Each wild zone has a **wild boss** that a single colony can't beat. The ladder is by colonies joined (section 3).

| # | Zone | Where | Wild boss | Needs | The fight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Ditch** | the south row's west half: the wet edge, drain and mudflat | **The Duke**, a bullfrog three times Old Warty | 3 colonies | Swallows whole crews (up to 6 in one gulp); dives and resurfaces somewhere else in the ditch; its tongue sweeps a lane. Pin it with 30 biters and it can't dive. |
| 2 | **The Bramble Lot** | the west column: brambles over the side yard and round the shed | **Old Black**, a crow | 3 colonies | Lands, struts, and steals: it carries ants off, and glowrock from outposts. Out of reach in the air. A **shiny decoy** (a crew carrying a bottle cap) brings it down; then it's a brawl. |
| 3 | **The Hollow** | the east column: deep in the hedge row | **The Cordyceps Colony** | 4 colonies | A dead ant colony the fungus has taken: zombie ants pour out in waves, and spore clouds infect the living. At its heart, a fungal stalk. Medics cure the infected; you need waves of fighters to reach the heart. |
| 4 | **The Oak's Crown** | up the Oak's trunk | **The Hornet Queen** | 4 colonies | Her paper nest in the Oak's hollow. Hornets dive at anything on the bark. Crews climb the trunk in columns. Win, and her comb is full of larvae: the meat jackpot. |
| 5 | **The Sinkhole** | the far south-east corner: once the fire ants' queen falls, the ground by their old mound caves in | **Old Blind**, a mole the glow has changed (final) | the Supercolony | Where the ground caved in, the glowing seam lies open (the fire ants nested on it: that's why they were so fierce). A mole has been eating the grubs that feed on it for years, and grown huge, half-blind and wrong. It tunnels under the fight and erupts beneath crews; the open seam burns ants who linger. The last fight of the yard tier. |

**Wild prizes** (held like yard regions, when their boss falls):
- **The Ditch:** water never runs short; mud lets builders seal a cave-in instantly.
- **The Bramble Lot:** brambles shelter outposts (no birds).
- **The Hollow:** your colony is immune to cordyceps for good.
- **The Oak's Crown:** hornet larvae, a steady 3 meat a minute. That finally answers the meat ceiling, at the very end.
- **The Sinkhole:** an open glowrock seam (one every 5 minutes). And beating Old Blind starts **Phase 2** (section 4).

## 3. Musters: the macro layer

The "three colonies" rule needs a mechanic, and it's also where the game turns macro, as Mark described.

**A muster** is a call to arms from the map:
- Tap a wild boss's lair on the map and choose **Muster**. Each colony you pick (main nest, any joined daughter) sends a contingent of its fighters. The War Camp sends the most, and the Brood Hall its big castes.
- **They march on their own,** through tunnels to the daughter nearest the fight, then overland. You watch the columns on the map.
- **Wild bosses can't be fought without a muster** of at least as many colonies as their tier needs. Their lair doesn't wake for less.
- **You lead it.** The fight is on the surface as now: your ant, your crew and the columns. You can still use your abilities and crew orders.

**Standing orders for daughters** (the "main nest no longer needs me" feel):
- Each daughter gets an order on the map: **Gather**, **Guard**, or **Ready**. Ready means her fighters are on call for musters.
- The main nest gets the same.
- The game nudges you there: "Your War Camp is idle: set her to Ready?"

This keeps the game hands-on (you still fight in person) but lets you think in colonies.

## 4. How you get there

- **The edges stir** when your **third daughter is joined**. A banner: "Something stirs at the yard's edges." The outer ring turns wild, and the Ditch and the Bramble Lot wake (3 colonies). Green cracks show where the seams break the surface.
- **The Hollow and the Oak's Crown** need 4 colonies (main + 3 daughters).
- **The Sinkhole** needs the Supercolony and the fire ants' queen beaten (her mound is where the ground caves in).
- **Beating Old Blind starts Phase 2** (Mark: "when we beat it, it prompts the house takeover, and the evolution prompting the second takeover, Phase 2"):
  - the colony, glow-fed from Old Blind's seam, is offered one last evolution, **the Takeover**: the turn from yard colony to house invader;
  - evolving it plays the ending: the camera rises over the yard, then the house's back door swings open;
  - Phase 2, the house, begins.
- **The 13-guardian ending changes:** taking all 13 zones becomes "the yard is yours", a celebration with your colony's record, but the back door now opens only after Old Blind. The wilds are the road to the house, not a side trip.

**A suggested order:**
1. The edges stir, and musters (one build: the wild rules need something to fight with).
2. The Ditch and the Duke (the first wild boss, proving the muster).
3. The Bramble Lot and Old Black.
4. The Hollow.
5. The Oak's Crown.
6. The Sinkhole, Old Blind and the Takeover (the hand-off to Phase 2).

## 6. Decided (Nov 4)

1. **The yard's own outer ring:** the west column, east column and south row, inside the fence. No new ground; the edges turn wild once you have three colonies.
2. **Musters and standing orders** as the macro layer.
3. **The bosses:** the Duke, Old Black, the Cordyceps Colony, the Hornet Queen; **Old Blind** replaces the Thing in the Drum.
4. **The Oak's Crown** is climbable.
5. **The order:** the wilds come before the house. Beating Old Blind offers the Takeover evolution, which opens Phase 2.
